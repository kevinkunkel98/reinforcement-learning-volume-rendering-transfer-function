# 2026-10-17: task v2 -- making hidden-organ instructions solvable

Follow-up to [2026-10-16](2026-10-16-overnight-results.md), which ended with
the pure model-based policy at a plateau (~0.2 median) that more data,
patients and policy capacity no longer moved, and about 30 % of val
instructions that no method -- not even a 1000-evaluation search -- could
improve. This asks why, and changes the task definition so that every
instruction kind is achievable. The v1 task stays available unchanged
(`task_version="v1"`, the default); v2 is opt-in.

## Why instructions were unsolvable

At the v1 start transfer function the unlabelled tissue ("other": skin,
fat, unsegmented muscle) makes up 40-99 % of the image; the eight goal
classes together 0.1-10 %. Unlabelled voxels are shaded by the skeleton,
lungs and soft peaks (`transfer.UNLABELLED_PEAKS`), so that shell sits in
front of everything. Looking at the unsolvable episodes one by one:

| episode | instruction | why nothing helps |
|---|---|---|
| 50000 | "a lot less lungs" | lungs already at vis 0.000 -- nothing to lower |
| 50011 | "strongly less lungs, a bit less spleen" | both already ~0 |
| 50002 | "more spleen, a lot more kidneys" | hidden behind the shell; clearing it reveals them (0.03 / 0.05), but from the EPSILON floor that is +1.5 log10, not the +0.3 / +0.6 asked -- overshoot is charged, so revealing them scores *worse* than doing nothing, and fading the shell costs keep-drift on top |
| 50009 | "a lot more bone, more lungs" | the skeleton peak also lights unlabelled tissue, "other" stays at 0.51 |

So most "unsolvable" instructions are ill-posed, not physically impossible.

## Four layers, measured before any training

Ceiling proxy: cold `hill_climb` with 500 evaluations, 100 val episodes
(seeds 50 000+), median attainment
(`2026-10-17-scripts/solvability_probe.py`):

| setting | overall | absolute | brightness | compound | relative | show_only | solvable |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v1 (baseline) | 0.49 | 0.53 | 0.99 | 0.26 | 0.75 | 0.16 | 48 % |
| visible organs | 0.49 | 0.61 | 0.99 | 0.43 | 0.66 | 0.39 | 60 % |
| occlusion-aware | 0.55 | 0.82 | 0.99 | 0.46 | 0.81 | 0.16 | 48 % |
| well-posed | 0.61 | 0.35 | 0.99 | 0.61 | 0.92 | 0.16 | 100 % |
| reachable | 0.72 | 0.53 | 0.99 | 0.58 | 0.85 | 0.24 | |
| visible + occl | 0.64 | 0.94 | 0.99 | 0.46 | 0.86 | 0.39 | 60 % |
| visible + occl + well-posed | 0.73 | 0.93 | 0.99 | 0.62 | 0.90 | 0.39 | 100 % |
| all four | 0.81 | 0.93 | 0.99 | 0.72 | 0.90 | 0.47 | 100 % |
| **all four, occl. also for show_only (= task v2)** | **0.82** | **0.93** | **0.99** | **0.72** | **0.90** | **0.56** | **100 %** |

(n per kind ≈ 14-17 absolute, 9 brightness, 21-23 compound, 37 relative,
17 show_only; the instructions differ between settings that change the
start or the sampler.) Each layer fixes a different kind; together every
kind's achievable median is above 0.5.

1. **Visible organs at the start** (`transfer.VISIBLE_ORGAN_HEIGHTS`): soft
   tissue (which also shades the shell) nearly transparent, organs at 0.45.
   The viewer would open on a transfer function where the organs a user
   might ask about are already in view.
2. **Well-posed instructions** (`solvable_only` sampling, always on in v2):
   no "less X" when X is already invisible, no "more X" from the floor.
3. **Occlusion-aware keep terms** (`goals.find_occluders`,
   `goals.occlusion_aware`): when an instruction asks to make a class more
   visible, the classes in front of it -- found by removal, alone and
   jointly, including the unlabelled shell -- are exempt from the keep
   terms. Fading what hides a target is what was asked for, not drift. In 7
   of 17 show_only episodes the shell was the occluder.
4. **Reachable targets** (`goals.reachable_targets`): each visibility target
   clipped to what the class can reach from the start -- not below the
   EPSILON floor, not above its single-peak ceiling.

All four are expressed through the goal vector and `weights` (an occluder
is "mentioned with weight 0"; an occluding shell is `weights["other"] = 0`,
which `goals.distance` now reads), so every scorer -- the env, `hill_climb`,
`vis_eval`'s baselines, the model-based trainer's torch twin -- scores v2
without its own code path.

## Model-based RL on task v2

Same pipeline as v1 (`rl.model_based collect/train --task-version v2`), 90
training patients (`train,train_extra`), 200 val episodes (seeds 50 000+),
policy alone, each seed's own median:

| | seed 0 | seed 1 | seed 2 | model predicts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| round 0 (3000 random starts) | 0.155 | 0.136 | 0.044 | ~0.6 |
| round 1 (+1500 Dyna starts) | 0.345 | 0.369 | 0.379 | ~0.6 |
| round 2 (+1500) | 0.385 | 0.388 | 0.376 | ~0.6 |
| **round 2 + closed-loop training, step 1** | **0.461** | **0.438** | **0.477** | |
| ... step 2 (one re-render) | 0.414 | 0.392 | 0.433 | |

The first v2 run trained on the wrong instructions: `collect_starts` drew
its per-start training instructions from the bare grammar, so the policy
learned v1-style goals and was scored on v2 ones (model +0.44, real −0.02).
Fixed in 7278d8b (one `OneShotEnv.sample_real_instruction` for reset and
the collector); the numbers above are after the fix.

"Closed-loop training" is `2026-10-16-scripts/mb_multistep.py` with
`TASK_VERSION=v2`: the policy is trained through a 2-step rollout in the
learned model (each step sees the remaining goal; v2 occluders stay free),
20k steps from the round-2 checkpoints. Its first step -- a plain one-shot
policy -- is the best pure policy; the second step helps relative (0.53-0.57
→ 0.59-0.67) and costs show_only (0.26-0.29 → 0.05-0.12), so it loses
overall.

By kind, best configuration (step 1), against the v2 ceiling:

| kind | policy (3 seeds) | ceiling (500-eval search) |
|---|---|---|
| brightness | 0.77 – 0.78 | 0.99 |
| compound | 0.55 – 0.57 | 0.72 |
| relative | 0.53 – 0.57 | 0.90 |
| absolute | 0.35 – 0.39 | 0.93 |
| show_only | 0.26 – 0.29 | 0.56 |

Pure RL path: SAC 0.00 → model-based v1 0.19 → v1 closed loop 0.24 → v2
0.37 → v2 with closed-loop training **0.46**. Three of five kinds are above
0.5 in one step; absolute has the most room left (0.37 of 0.93), show_only
the least ceiling (0.56).

The v1 and v2 numbers are not directly comparable -- v2 is a different
task, by design -- so the thesis reports both: v1 to show RL against the
original definition, v2 to show what the same learner reaches once the
instructions are well-posed.
